Our Story

Born in the hills. Built for the hills.

Mizo Travel began with a simple frustration. Our founders kept watching visitors arrive in Aizawl with a printed itinerary that hit every landmark and missed everything that mattered — the family that makes the best bai in Thenzawl, the ridge above Hmuifang that no guide book mentions, the way Chapchar Kut changes a village in a single morning.

"Every rupee you spend with us stays in Mizoram — with the people who live here, not a booking platform in another city."

We are not an outsourced vendor. We are not a call centre. When you send us a message, a person from Aizawl reads it and replies — usually the same day. When you travel with us, your guide grew up in or near the district you're visiting. We've walked every route we sell.

We started small — two guides, a handful of families willing to host guests, and a conviction that Mizoram deserved better representation in Northeast India tourism. Today, 500+ travellers later, that conviction hasn't changed. What's changed is how many families we've been able to bring along with us.

How we got here

  1. 2018

    The first group

    Our founders lead their first small group through Reiek Tlang and Phawngpui. Two guides, one car, six guests — and a WhatsApp group that's still active today.

  2. 2019

    First village partnership

    We formalise our first three village homestay partnerships in Thenzawl and Champhai, ensuring fair income stays directly with host families.

  3. 2021

    Ministry of Tourism recognition

    Registered with MSME and recognised by the Ministry of Tourism, Government of India. Inner Line Permit assistance formalised as a standard part of every booking.

  4. 2023

    100+ guests — and counting

    We cross 100 hosted travellers and expand to all 9 districts of Mizoram. Chapchar Kut festival trail added as a dedicated seasonal package.

  5. Today

    500+ happy travellers, same values

    21 village partner families. 25+ local guide partners. A 5.0 Google rating. And the same reason we started: showing guests the Mizoram we actually live in.

What we stand for

The principles every trip is built on.

These aren't marketing promises — they're the decisions we make every time we build an itinerary or choose a guide.

01

Locally rooted, not locally branded

We live and work in Mizoram. Our decisions about routes, partners and pricing are made here — not in a mainland head office that's never been to Phawngpui.

02

We've walked every route we sell

No itinerary gets listed unless our team has done it. If a trail is blocked after the monsoon or a village homestay has closed, we already know before you book.

03

Small groups — always

We keep group sizes small so you're a guest in a village, not a crowd passing through. Bigger groups have better options elsewhere. We're fine with that.

04

Money stays in Mizoram

Every rupee you spend goes to local guides, partner families, and community-run experiences — not to middlemen or platforms elsewhere. That's not an accident; it's the model.

05

Guides from the district you visit

Every route is led by someone who grew up near it. They know the shortcuts, the seasonal closures, the family names along the trail — and the real history behind each site.

06

Culture is not a performance

We don't stage Mizo culture for photos. When you join a Chapchar Kut celebration or sit down in a village kitchen, it's because the family wanted you there — not because we arranged it for tourism.

The people behind the trips

Guides who grew up here. Hosts who mean it.

Our team isn't assembled from a freelance directory. Every guide we work with has a personal connection to the territory they lead — either they grew up there, their family lives there, or they've spent years learning its trails and communities from the inside.

We believe this is the difference between a good trip and the kind that changes how you think about travel. You can't fake local knowledge, and we've never tried to.

Before every trip, your guide will introduce themselves — not with a credentials sheet, but usually with a voice note and a question about what you're most hoping to see. It's how we've always done it. Our guests tend to leave with their guide's personal number.

Tlawmngaihna — the Mizo word for selfless hospitality — isn't something we teach in training. It's something our team grew up with.

Responsible Tourism

Tourism that the hills can actually carry.

Mizoram doesn't need more tourists. It needs better tourism — travel that adds something to the communities it passes through instead of extracting from them. That's been our operating principle since the beginning.

We work exclusively with village homestays, local transport, and community-run experiences. We set and enforce group size limits on sensitive trekking routes. We don't take bookings to restricted areas without the proper permits in place. And we say no to itineraries that are clearly built for a photo, not a place.

Talk to our team

Village-first partnerships

Accommodation, meals, and local activities are purchased directly from village families — never through an intermediary that skims the margin.

Low-impact trail practices

Group caps on every trekking route, no single-use plastics on trails, and a carry-in carry-out policy we actually enforce with our own groups.

Permit compliance, always

Inner Line Permits and Protected Area Permits are arranged as part of every booking — not an optional add-on. We don't take guests into restricted zones unprepared.

Cultural respect as a booking condition

Before every trip, guests receive a short brief on Mizo customs. It's not a lecture — it's how you ensure the host families actually want you back.
