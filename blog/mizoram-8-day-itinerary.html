Eight days is the right number for exploring Mizoram. Long enough to reach Thenzawl in the south, slow enough to spend a real evening in Champhai, and tight enough that you won't spend half the trip in a car. This travel guide outlines the exact Mizoram tour itinerary we run as our Highlands Discovery package — refined over dozens of trips and adjusted here for first-time visitors who want the full picture of the state.

Overview & quick facts

Before the day-by-day, here's the frame. This route covers the central and southern districts of Mizoram — the most scenically diverse corridor in the state. You'll spend time in Aizawl (the capital), cross into the eastern highlands via Champhai, dip south to Thenzawl, and loop back through Hmuifang.

Know before you plan (Essential Mizoram Travel Guide)

Planning a Mizoram tour itinerary requires a bit more preparation than mainstream destinations. Here are the non-negotiable details to sort out before booking your 8-day Mizoram tour package.

  • The Sunday Shutdown: Mizoram is a deeply Christian state. From Saturday evening to Monday morning, virtually everything closes. Markets, restaurants, taxis, and inter-city transport completely stop. Use Sundays strictly for nature walks or hotel rest. Do not plan travel days on a Sunday.
  • Inner Line Permits (ILP): All Indian tourists outside Mizoram require an ILP [1]. You can apply online via the official Mizoram e-ILP portal or get it on arrival at Lengpui Airport [2]. Foreign nationals require a Protected Area Permit (PAP).
  • Cash is King outside Aizawl: While Aizawl runs smoothly on UPI, connectivity in Champhai, Zokhawthar, and rural homestays is spotty. Withdraw sufficient cash in Aizawl for your eastern and southern circuits.
  • Dry State Realities: Mizoram is a dry state. Alcohol is strictly prohibited and carrying it across borders is illegal. Respect local laws and avoid carrying liquor in your luggage.
  • Tlawmngaihna (The Mizo Code): This is the cultural cornerstone of Mizoram — putting others before oneself. You will experience this through extreme hospitality, honesty, and safety. Bargaining aggressively is considered rude here.

How to reach Mizoram: Flights Options & Train Options

Planning your transit is a crucial step. Here are the best transportation modes to begin your 8-day Mizoram tour:

By Air (Flights to Aizawl)

The fastest way to reach Mizoram is by flying into Lengpui Airport (AJL), located about 30 km from the Aizawl city centre. Direct flights are available from major regional hubs:

  • Kolkata (CCU): Daily direct flights via IndiGo and Air India (Approx. 1 hr 15 mins).
  • Guwahati (GAU): Frequent direct and connecting flights (Approx. 1 hr).
  • Imphal & Agartala: Regional connections frequently serviced by Alliance Air.
  • Delhi (DEL) & Mumbai (BOM): Excellent connecting flights routed primarily through Kolkata or Guwahati.

By Train

Sairang Railway Station: Located just 20 km from Aizawl, this newly developing railhead connects Mizoram directly to the broader Indian railway network via the Bairabi-Sairang line. We offer direct pickups from this station.

  • Sairang – Anand Vihar Terminal Rajdhani Express (20507 / 20508): Premium long-distance connectivity linking Mizoram directly to New Delhi.
  • Sairang – Kolkata Express (13125 / 13126): Regular express service connecting Sairang to Kolkata (Howrah/Kolkata terminal network).
  • Guwahati – Sairang Express (15609 / 15610): Overnight or daily connective link between Assam’s primary hub and Mizoram.
  • Sairang – Silchar Passenger (55669 / 55670): Local regional commuter passenger train connecting neighboring Silchar to Sairang.

Day 1

Arrive in Aizawl — land, breathe, orient

Your first evening in the capital is deliberately slow. Aizawl deserves more than a rushed arrival and an early bed — there's a particular quality to the light here at dusk that no photo has ever quite captured.

Airport / Station → Aizawl
01
OvernightAizawl
Drive35 km
MealsDinner
PaceEasy

We pick you up from Lengpui Airport (or Sairang Railway Station if you are arriving by train). The 35-km mountain drive to Aizawl winds through spectacular ridgeline roads — the panoramic views of Mizoram's undulating green hills start immediately and set the tone for your 8-day Mizoram tour itinerary.

Depending on your arrival time, your first day in the capital unfolds in one of two ways. If you land in the early morning, we whisk you straight out for an immersive sightseeing introduction: start with the vertigo-inducing heights of the Aizawl Peak Skywalk for sprawling bird's-eye valley views, pay a visit to the historic and poignant Hángi Lunglên Tlâng (the hill of the weeping heart), and wind down with locally roasted specialty coffee at the trendy 14 Beans Cafe before checking into your hotel.

If your flight or train arrives in the afternoon or evening, we head straight to your hotel for a smooth check-in. Afterward, stroll down to Bara Bazar — the bustling central market that spills dramatically down the hillside — to experience authentic local commerce, vibrant traditional textiles, and your first cup of hot tea with your guide while the city lights begin to flicker across the ridges. Dinner is served at a cozy local restaurant nearby; we'll gladly order a traditional Mizo spread for you to introduce your palate to local delicacies like Bai before the full expedition begins.

Get a good night's rest. Day 2 starts promptly at 8:00 AM as we journey eastward into the heart of the Mizo highlands toward Champhai.

Day 2

Aizawl to Champhai — via serene Tam Dil

The 193-km drive from Aizawl to Champhai takes 6–7 hours and passes through some of the most extraordinary terraced landscapes in Northeast India, including a tranquil mid-journey stop at Tam Dil lake.

Aizawl → Champhai
02
OvernightMualnuam, Champhai
Drive193 km
Duration6-7 Hours
PaceScenic & Relaxed

Start your Aizawl to Champhai drive at 8am as the scenic highway climbs steadily through spectacular terraced hill country. En route, we take a slight detour near Saitual to visit Tam Dil (Lake of Mustard). Surrounded by lush virgin forests, this tranquil natural lake is the perfect mid-journey break on your Mizoram tour. We'll spend some time here enjoying the serene waters, stretching our legs, and having a peaceful lakeside lunch immersed in nature.

After Tam Dil, the journey continues with dramatic road sections—navigating long switchback descents into deep river gorges, followed by steep climbs back to ridge elevation. Arriving in the district by late afternoon, our first stop is the famous viewpoint at Champhai Mualnuam. Sitting on an elevated plateau looking east toward Myanmar, the golden evening light on the rolling border hills is absolutely breathtaking. Following the sunset, we will check into our Champhai hotel to freshen up. The evening is yours to step out and explore the surprisingly vibrant nightlife of Champhai, where you can wander through bustling evening markets, taste local Mizo street food, and experience the lively culture of this thriving border town.

Road conditions: The mountain highways are generally well-maintained, but sections can wash out after heavy rain. During peak monsoon (July–August), this drive can take 8+ hours. We check road conditions 48 hours before departure and adjust the itinerary where necessary.

Day 3

Border run — Zokhawthar & the heart-shaped Rih Dil

A drive from Champhai to the India-Myanmar border town of Zokhawthar, followed by a cross-border visit to the culturally significant, heart-shaped Rih Dil lake in Myanmar, before returning to Aizawl.

Champhai → Zokhawthar
03
OvernightAizawl
BorderIndia-Myanmar
HighlightRih Dil Lake
PaceActive / Driving

Early departure from Champhai for the drive to Zokhawthar, the bustling border town sitting on the edge of India and Myanmar. The road winds through the borderlands, offering views of the rugged terrain that separates the two nations. At Zokhawthar, we observe the cross-border trade and the unique blend of cultures at this frontier.

From Zokhawthar, we cross the international border (subject to permits and current regulations) into Myanmar to visit the legendary Rih Dil. This natural, heart-shaped lake is deeply revered in Mizo folklore as the passageway for spirits to the afterlife. It's a surreal experience to visit a place so culturally central to Mizoram, yet physically located in another country.

After spending time at the lake and grabbing lunch near the border, we begin the long but scenic return journey all the way back to Aizawl. Expect to arrive late in the evening, checking into your hotel in Aizawl for a well-deserved rest.

Rih Dil Lake & Myanmar Border Crossing: Officially, the international border crossing into Myanmar is currently closed to tourists. However, an alternative, off-beat route is accessible when the river's water level drops. During these periods, adventurous travelers can cross the border by scooty without formal permission to visit the legendary, heart-shaped Rih Dil Lake. Please note that this scooty excursion is entirely optional and incurs an additional cost of ₹800 per person.

Day 4

Reiek Tlang — the half-day trek most visitors skip

Reiek is 30 km from Aizawl and 20 minutes from the road. The trek itself takes three hours. The traditional village at the top is one of the few in Mizoram that's been properly reconstructed with real craftspeople involved.

Aizawl → Reiek Tlang → Aizawl
04
OvernightAizawl
Trek~8 km round trip
Elevation~1,600 m
PaceModerate

Get an early start with a scenic morning drive from Aizawl. The picturesque route toward Reiek is a destination in itself, taking you past the sweeping bends of the majestic Tlawng River and offering a refreshing stop at the lush Vaipuanpho Waterfalls—perfect early-morning photo opportunities to kick off your Mizoram itinerary.

By 8:00 AM, arrive at the Reiek Heritage Village near the trailhead. This beautifully reconstructed traditional Mizo settlement serves as a living museum of pre-Christian tribal life. Explore the distinctive architecture as you walk among the thatched-roof longhouses, the communal granary, the chief's residence, and the zawlbuk (bachelor's dormitory), getting a deep dive into the region's rich cultural history.

From the village, the trek through the Reiek Ecological Park begins. The trail climbs steadily through dense, vibrant forests of pine and bamboo before breaking out onto an exposed rocky ridge. Here, you are rewarded with the dramatic Reiek Tlang viewpoints. Standing on the sheer cliff edges, you'll be treated to unmatched panoramic views of the sprawling Sairang valley below and, on a clear day, the endless rolling hills stretching toward the eastern plateaus.

After soaking in the summit views, descend the peak and enjoy a relaxing drive back, returning to Aizawl by 2:00 PM. Your afternoon is completely free—take time to rest, explore the bustling local streets and cafes of Aizawl, and close out this chapter of your trip before heading south tomorrow.

Trailhead Access & Transport: All vehicles arriving from Aizawl will drop you at the main Reiek droppoint (near the Heritage Village). From this point, you have two options to reach the upper viewpoint where your actual Reiek trek begins. You can enjoy a scenic 20-minute walk through the ecological park, which serves as a great warm-up, or opt for a local cab transfer directly to the hiking starting point. The vehicle drop-off costs a fixed rate of ₹300 per car—a convenient choice if you want to save your energy for the summit climb.

Day 5

Aizawl to Hmuifang — Meadows, mountains & heritage

A stunning southward drive from Aizawl covering the cultural Falkawn village, the Tropic of Cancer, dramatic viewpoints, and the rolling green hills of Sialsuk, ending in the peaceful town of Thenzawl.

Aizawl → Hmuifang → Thenzawl
05
OvernightThenzawl
Drive~95 km
HighlightSialsuk Meadows
PaceScenic

Departing Aizawl and heading south, our first stop is Falkawn Mizo Village, a meticulously crafted model village that brings traditional Mizo history, architecture, and daily life into focus. A short drive further along the highway brings us to the Tropic of Cancer marker, a classic geographical photo op on this route.

The road continues to climb, leading to the breathtaking Midum Kham viewpoint, where steep cliffs drop away into endless valleys. From here, we ascend to Hmuifang Tlang. At over 1,600 metres, this mountain is wrapped in virgin forests and features beautifully maintained grassy ridges that have hosted traditional Mizo festivals for generations.

In the late afternoon, we reach the highlight of the day: the Sialsuk meadows. These rolling green hillocks offer 360-degree panoramic views of the sprawling Mizo hills — a perfect spot to watch the shifting afternoon light. Finally, we descend into the valley to the peaceful handloom hub of Thenzawl, checking into our accommodation for the night.

Cultural Experience: If your Mizoram travel itinerary aligns with a weekend, highly consider a visit to the Falkawn Mizo Village. Held exclusively every Saturday, this model village transforms into an immersive live museum. For a ticket price of just ₹100 per person, you can witness authentic traditional Mizo culture, local attire, tribal dances, and historical reenactments. Timing your Aizawl sightseeing to include a Saturday visit here offers an unforgettable, budget-friendly deep dive into the region's rich tribal heritage.

Day 6

Thenzawl Exploration — Waterfalls, parks & a return to Aizawl

Spend the day discovering Thenzawl’s natural and recreational highlights—from the towering Vantawng Falls and unique Tuirihau cascade to the rolling greens of the local Golf Course—before heading back to the capital.

Thenzawl → Return to Aizawl
06
OvernightAizawl
Drive~110 km
HighlightsWaterfalls & Parks
PaceActive / Scenic

Our morning in Thenzawl begins with its spectacular waterfalls. We first visit the unique Tuirihau waterfall, a beautiful cascade that you can actually walk behind, offering a stunning perspective from inside the cave-like recess. Just a short drive away is Vantawng Falls, Mizoram's tallest waterfall, plunging 229 metres in three magnificent tiers through a dense bamboo forest gorge.

Next, we explore the recreational side of the town. We'll take a peaceful stroll through Dilpui Park, a tranquil spot surrounded by lush nature. Afterwards, we visit the beautifully maintained Thenzawl Golf Course — one of the most scenic in Northeast India. Adjacent to it is a charming Deer Park where you can spot deer grazing peacefully on the open greens.

In the late afternoon, we bid farewell to Thenzawl and begin our scenic drive back north towards the capital. Enjoy the shifting evening light across the hills, eventually checking back into your hotel in Aizawl for the night.

Travel note: Tuirihau is unique in Mizoram as the only major waterfall where you can safely step directly behind the falling water. Make sure to wear shoes with good grip, as the rocky path near the falls can be quite slippery.

Day 7

Aizawl Exploration — Panoramas, heritage & coffee

A full day dedicated to the capital city. From the striking heights of the Aizawl Peak Skywalk to the historical depths of the State Museum, capped off with Mizoram's finest local coffee culture.

Aizawl Local Sightseeing
07
OvernightAizawl
Drive~40 km (City)
HighlightsSkywalk & Heritage
PaceRelaxed

Your morning starts with a refreshing visit to Lalsavunga Park, offering fantastic panoramic views of the rolling hills to start the day. From there, we head to Solomon's Temple — a magnificent white marble edifice that is celebrated not just for religion, but for its striking architecture and serene courtyard overlooking the sprawling city below.

Next, we dive into local history at the Mizoram State Museum, which houses the state's best collection of traditional Mizo tools, textiles, and historical documentation. Alternatively (or additionally), we visit KV Paradise, often referred to as the Taj Mahal of Mizoram, offering a poignant local story paired with sweeping valley views.

In the late afternoon, experience the thrill of the Aizawl Peak Skywalk, where elevated viewpoints offer vertigo-inducing perspectives of the city perched implausibly on steep mountain ridges. Unwind afterward at the popular 14 Beans Cafe, sampling locally sourced Mizo coffee as you watch the city lights flicker to life. If you complete these Day 1 sightseeing activities early, dive right into the vibrant Aizawl city nightlife and head to the bustling Bara Bazar for shopping—the city's primary commercial hub and the perfect spot to hunt for traditional Mizo garments, handicrafts, and local street food. If time runs short and you can't fit it all in on Day 1, don't worry; you can easily bundle the Skywalk, cafe visit, and your Bara Bazar shopping spree into a later afternoon on your Mizoram itinerary.

Museum timing: The Mizoram State Museum is closed on Sundays. If Day 7 falls on a Sunday, we will swap this visit with an extended tour of local hillside neighbourhoods or the Bara Bazar (if open earlier in the trip).

Mizoram Festival Calendar (Kût)

Mizoram's festivals are deeply tied to its agricultural cycles. Travellers visiting during the golden autumn months of October and November are in for a special treat, as this window hosts some of the state's most vibrant cultural showcases. Time your tour around these dates for a spectacular cultural bonus featuring Cheraw (bamboo dance), traditional music, and Mizo hospitality:

  • Chapchar Kut (Early March): The most famous festival, celebrating the clearing of the forests for jhum cultivation. Expect massive city-wide celebrations and the most vibrant Cheraw dance performances.
  • Anthurium Festival (October): Usually held in Reiek. Promotes tourism and flower cultivation against the backdrop of the stunning autumn mountains. Great for music, local food, and cultural displays.
  • Hnahshin Kût (October): A beautiful autumn festival celebrated primarily in the Champhai region, marking the changing colours of the leaves (Hnahshin). It features traditional Mizo dances, handloom exhibitions, and local feasts amidst the eastern border hills.
  • Mim Kut (August/September): A festival dedicated to the maize harvest and paying respects to departed souls. It has a slightly more solemn but deeply cultural atmosphere.
  • Thalfavang Kut (November): Marks the completion of weeding the fields. A brilliant time to witness traditional games and community feasts.
  • Pawl Kut (December): The post-harvest festival celebrating a successful year. Filled with meat feasts, traditional rice beer, and joyful thanksgiving.

Planning tips from our guides

What to pack

  • Layers: Mornings are cold at altitude year-round. A fleece + windproof shell covers most conditions outside the monsoon.
  • Trekking shoes: Mandatory for Day 4. Trail runners work; waterproof soles are better.
  • Power bank: Charging points in remote areas and homestays can be unreliable.
  • Cash (INR): ATMs thin out past Champhai. Carry enough for 3–4 days at minimum.
  • Reusable water bottle: We refill at safe sources en route — single-use plastic is discouraged on all our routes.

Connectivity

Airtel has the best rural coverage in Mizoram. BSNL works in most towns. Expect dead zones between Champhai and Zokhawthar. Download offline maps (Google Maps works) for the full route before you leave Aizawl. Your guide will always have a local SIM with a working signal.

Pro tips and local secrets

Over the years of running our signature Mizoram itinerary, we've gathered a few inside secrets to elevate your road trip:

  • Start your days early: Mizoram runs on Northeast time, even though it follows standard IST. The sun rises by 4:45 AM and sets by 4:45 PM. Start your drives by 7:00 AM to maximize daylight.
  • Book an experienced hill driver: The mountain roads are beautiful but demand respect. Heavy fog and tight switchbacks are common. All our Mizoram tour packages include drivers native to these specific districts.
  • Eat local: Skip the standard paneer and chicken curry. Ask your homestays for Bai (mixed vegetable stew), Vawksa Rep (smoked pork), and Sawhchiar (rice cooked with meat). And don't leave without trying locally grown Mizo coffee.
  • Discover Mizoram Tourism initiatives: The official Mizoram Tourism website [1] frequently posts local festival dates (like Anthurium or Chapchar Kut) that can dramatically improve your timing.
